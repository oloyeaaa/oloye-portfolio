## Why the reviews don't come in

You finish the work. They're pleased. You mean to ask for a review, and then the next customer comes in. By the time you remember, the moment's gone.

Asking in person can feel awkward too. A message that goes out on its own takes the awkward bit away.

## Automate the Google review request

1. **Get your review link.** In your [Google Business Profile](https://www.google.com/business/), Google gives you a link (and a QR code) that takes customers straight to the review box.
2. **Mark the work done** in your client list in [Airtable](https://www.airtable.com/), or let your invoice or booking app do it.
3. **A day later, one short email goes out** from your Gmail, with the link. [Make](https://www.make.com/) joins the steps.
4. **No review after a week?** One gentle reminder. Then it stops. Nobody gets pestered.
5. **Every finished customer gets the same ask.** Not only the ones you think will be happy. That's Google's rule (see below), and it's fairer.

## Google review request email template

> Hi [name],
>
> Thanks for choosing [business]. If you've got a minute, a short Google review would really help others find us: [review link]
>
> Thanks,
> [your name]

Short, one link, one ask. No "5 stars please".

## How to ask for Google reviews from customers, the right way

Google's policy says businesses must not:

- **Ask only happy customers.** The policy calls it selectively soliciting positive reviews.
- **Discourage or block bad reviews.**
- **Give anything in return**, like a discount or a free item.
- **Pressure people** to leave one while they're on the premises.

Read [Google's policy on fake and misleading content](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114) before you start. An automated ask that goes to everyone, with nothing in return, keeps you inside it.

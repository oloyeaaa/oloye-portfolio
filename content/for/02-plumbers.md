## The admin after the last job

You write the quote at 9pm. You send it. Then nothing. Was it too high? Did they go with someone else? You don't have time to ring round and ask.

You're not alone in this. In a 2026 survey of UK tradespeople:

- They spend about **5 hours 20 minutes a week** on quoting, invoicing, scheduling and chasing payments.
- **62%** do their admin in the evenings and **43%** at weekends.
- **55%** believe they've definitely or probably lost a job because a quote went out too slowly.
- On average they're owed **£5,901** in unpaid invoices, and **60%** have written off money because chasing it felt like too much hassle.

*([Powered Now NOW Report 2026](https://powerednow.com/mental-health-in-the-trades-2026/), with Installer: 140 tradespeople, April to May 2026. The admin and payment figures come from the 100 who do their own admin.)*

## Quote follow up automation

Here's the quote follow-up, built with tools that have free plans to start.

1. **You send the quote** the way you do now. Then you add one line to a simple list in [Airtable](https://www.airtable.com/): the customer, the work, the price, the date. A short form on your phone does it in a few taps.
2. **[Make](https://www.make.com/) watches the list.** It's the tool that passes information from one app to the next.
3. **No reply after a few days?** A friendly follow-up email goes out from your own Gmail. You pick how many days.
4. **Still quiet?** One more nudge a week later, then it stops. Nobody gets pestered.
5. **They say yes?** You tick "Booked", and the follow-ups stop.

The same list does the invoices. Tick a job "Done" and the invoice reminder goes out on the due date, politely, without you having to remember.

We built the same shape of workflow for our own enquiries first: [Website forms into one lead list](/case-studies/website-forms-to-lead-list). We haven't built it for a plumber yet. This is how it would be set up for quotes.

## Quote follow up email template

Copy this, or use it in the workflow above:

> Hi [name],
>
> Just checking you got the quote for [the work]. Happy to answer any questions, or I can pencil you in for [week].
>
> Thanks,
> [your name]

It's short on purpose. It asks one easy question, and it gives them a date.

## When you can't pick up

You can't answer the phone with your hands in a boiler. In a 2022 survey of over 220 tradespeople, **34%** believed they'd lost work by not answering, and **60%** said they struggle to answer while working. *([Fix Radio and Builders Station](https://www.fixradio.co.uk/fix-feed/construction-news/post/34-of-tradespeople-lose-work-by-not-answering-phone/), Oct 2022.)*

We don't build phone systems. What we can do: put a short "tell us what you need" form on your website, and link to it from your Google listing, so anyone who can't get through can leave their details. They get an email back straight away, and they land in the same list as your quotes.

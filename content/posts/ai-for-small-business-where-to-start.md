If you run a small business, you've heard it a hundred times: you should be using AI. What nobody tells you is where to start.

So you open a chat tool once, ask it something, get a bland answer, and close the tab. Nothing in your business changed.

Here's a more useful way in.

## Start with the task, not the tool

The mistake is starting with the technology. "Which AI tool should I use?" is the wrong first question. The right one is: **what eats my time every week?**

Make a short list. For most owners it looks like this:

- answering the same customer questions again and again
- following up quotes and enquiries that go quiet
- writing posts, emails and listings
- admin: invoices, reminders, follow-ups

Pick the one that costs you the most hours. That's your starting point.

## Where AI helps, and where simple tools do the work

AI is a tool inside the workflow, not the point of it. In most small businesses, the work splits into two parts:

### 1. The steps that are the same every time

Saving an enquiry, sending a welcome email, making a client folder, sending an invoice reminder. These don't need AI. Simple tools like [Make](https://www.make.com/) and [Airtable](https://www.airtable.com/) join your apps together, and the steps run on their own. Read how one runs in our own business: [daily task emails](/case-studies/daily-task-emails).

### 2. The steps that need words

A first draft of a reply, a summary of a long email, a caption from your notes. This is where [Claude](https://www.anthropic.com/claude) helps. It writes the first draft, and you check it before anything goes out.

Put the two together, and the repeat tasks run on their own while you still sign off the words.

## How to tell if an AI idea is worth it

One test: does it save you time, or help you make money? If it doesn't clearly do one of those, it's a toy, not a tool. Skip it.

That one question will save you from most of the hype.

## Don't try to do it all at once

The owners who get value from automation don't switch on ten things at once. They fix one painful task properly, feel the time come back, then move to the next.

## A simple first move

This week, pick the one task that wastes the most of your time. Just one. It's probably more obvious than you think.

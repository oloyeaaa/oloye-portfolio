Most small businesses don't lose work because they're bad at what they do. They lose it because nobody replied in time.

Someone messages three businesses at once. The first helpful reply usually wins. If you're on a ladder, driving or with a customer, that reply isn't yours.

Automating your customer enquiries fixes the part that doesn't need you. Here's how it works, in plain English.

## Why a fast reply matters

People shop around, and they don't wait long. Research in the [Harvard Business Review](https://hbr.org/2011/03/the-short-life-of-online-sales-leads) found that most companies don't respond to online leads nearly fast enough. A slow reply isn't rude. It's just late, and late often means someone else got there first.

So the goal is simple: every enquiry gets saved, and every enquiry gets a quick, friendly first reply, whatever you're doing.

## What "automating enquiries" really means

It doesn't mean a robot pretending to be you. It means a simple workflow that:

- saves every enquiry from your website form in one list
- sends a short first reply from your own email, so they know you've got it
- tells you who's still waiting, so the proper reply comes from you

You stay in charge. The workflow just makes sure nothing sits unanswered.

## The three pieces you need

### 1. One list for every enquiry

Right now your enquiries probably land in several places. The first task is to bring them into one. A list in [Airtable](https://www.airtable.com/) works well: one row per person, with where they came from and the date. That alone stops people slipping through the cracks.

### 2. A first reply that goes out on its own

When someone fills in your form, [Make](https://www.make.com/) passes their details to the list and sends a short reply from your Gmail. It's written once, in your words: thanks, here's what happens next, here's when you'll hear from us.

### 3. A daily look at who's waiting

A first reply buys you time. It doesn't win the work. So each morning, look at who's still waiting for a proper answer. Claude can draft each one for you to check and send.

## How to start without overthinking it

You don't need to automate everything on day one. Start with the place most of your enquiries come from, usually your website form. Get that saving and replying on its own. Then add the next.

A lot of people open [Claude](https://www.anthropic.com/claude), type "write me a post about my business" and press enter. What comes back is flat. It sounds like every other post out there, and nobody stops to read it.

That isn't Claude's fault. It was given nothing real to work with. Give it real words and clear rules, and it becomes a fast first-draft helper. That's how one person can get through the marketing work of a small team.

Here are the three ways we use it.

> **Good to know:** Claude does the first draft. You still decide what's true, what matters and what goes out. That part stays with you.

## Find the words your customers really use

Guessing what your customers worry about is hard. Reading what they've already written is much easier.

Reviews in your field are a good place to start, especially the 1 and 2 star ones. People say exactly what went wrong, in their own words. Copy 30 or 40 of them into Claude and ask it to sort them.

> **Try this:** paste the reviews under this prompt and see what comes back.

```text
Below are reviews from customers in my field.

Read them and pull out:
1. The 5 problems people mention most, in their own words. Quote them.
2. The one thing they most want to stop happening.
3. The everyday words they use to describe their day.

Keep their words exactly as they wrote them. Don't tidy them up.
```

What you get back is a short list of real phrases. Those make good first lines for a video, a post or the top of your website, because they sound like your customers and not like a brochure.

## Give Claude a few rules so the draft sounds like you

The robotic sound mostly comes from Claude guessing how you talk. So tell it. A few plain rules at the top of every request go a long way:

- Talk like someone who has done the job, explaining it to a friend.
- Cut the business words ("synergy", "empower", "paradigm shift").
- Use short sentences and everyday examples.

Save those rules somewhere you can copy them from, like a note on your phone or a Google Doc. Paste them in every time. Then add the real details: who the post is for, the problem, and what you actually did about it.

The draft still won't be perfect. Read it out loud. Anything you wouldn't say across a kitchen table, cut or change.

## Turn one recording into several pieces

This is where most of the time gets saved. One recording can feed a whole week.

### Step 1: Record and get a transcript

Talk through one idea on camera or as a voice note. Run it through a tool like [Descript](https://www.descript.com) or [Whisper](https://github.com/openai/whisper) to get the words as text.

### Step 2: Turn the transcript into a blog post

Give Claude the transcript and your rules. Ask it to clean up the spoken wander, add simple headings, and keep your own sentences where it can.

### Step 3: Pull out the short posts

Ask Claude for three short pieces from the same transcript, each one able to stand on its own. Those become posts for TikTok, Instagram or LinkedIn. Point each one back to your email list, because [followers on their own are hard to reach](/blog/why-10k-followers-mean-nothing-without-an-email-list).

> **In plain words:** repurposing just means using one thing you made in more than one place, instead of starting from scratch every time.

## Bring what you know, let Claude bring the speed

The trap here is thinking the tool will do the thinking. It won't. If you give it nothing, you get nothing much back.

What works is the other way round. You bring the real problems, the real words and the real story. Claude helps you get them down faster and in more places. Start with one recording this week and see how far it goes.

Someone asks about your services on Tuesday. You mean to reply. By Friday it feels awkward, and by next week you've let it go.

Quiet doesn't mean no. They might just be busy, the same as you.

This is how to follow up with leads before they go cold: reply the same day, follow a short plan, and keep everyone in one list, so remembering isn't down to you.

> **In plain words:** a lead is anyone who has shown interest in what you do. They might have filled in a form, sent a message or asked for a free guide.

## Why leads go cold so fast

The best-known study on this comes from [Harvard Business Review](https://hbr.org/2011/03/the-short-life-of-online-sales-leads). It looked at 2,241 US companies:

- 23% never replied to an online enquiry.
- The average reply took 42 hours.
- Replying within an hour made a firm about 7 times more likely to qualify the lead than waiting a further hour, and about 60 times more likely than waiting 24 hours or more.

To qualify a lead just means finding out they're a real buyer. That study is from 2011 and it's about companies with sales teams, not one-person businesses. So take the direction from it, not the exact numbers. Someone who asks today has you in mind today.

## How to follow up with leads: start with the first reply

Reply the same day, and within the hour if you can.

You can't always be there, so let a first reply go out on its own. We showed how we do that in [website forms to Airtable with Make](/blog/website-forms-to-airtable-with-make): someone asks for a free guide, and it goes straight to them.

Then answer properly yourself. Keep it to a line or two, thank them, and ask one question about them:

> Thanks for getting in touch, Sam. What made you look into this now?

That question matters more than anything you could say about yourself. It gives them something easy to answer.

## A follow-up plan you can copy

If they go quiet after that, here's a plan. Count the days from when they first got in touch.

![The follow-up plan: someone gets in touch and you reply the same day, then day 2 did it land, day 4 one useful thing, day 7 one question, day 14 the easy out, day 30 something new](/images/blog/how-to-follow-up-with-leads/follow-up-plan.png)

| Day | What to send | Why |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 2 | "Just checking that reached you OK. Any questions, reply here." | It shows there's a person here. |
| 4 | One useful tip on what they asked about. | You give before you ask. |
| 7 | "What's the one thing stopping you right now?" | They talk about their problem. |
| 14 | "No worries if now's not the time. Want me to check back in a month?" | Saying no gets easier than staying silent. |
| 30 | "I just made something on this. Want me to send it over?" | They get a new reason to reply. |

It's the plan we're setting up for our own leads, so we can't show you results from it yet. Send these manually first, in your own words. Once you know the words work, a tool can send them for you.

## How to follow up without being pushy

Being pushy usually means asking for something every time. So keep to a few simple rules:

- **One message at a time.** If they reply, stop the plan and just talk.
- **Keep it short.** If it doesn't fit on a phone screen, cut it.
- **Make it easy to say not now.** Either answer tells you where you stand.
- **If they say no, stop.**

> **Good to know:** these are one-to-one replies to someone who got in touch with you. Your email list is a separate thing. We only add people who ticked yes to emails, and we explained how in [getting every sign-up into one list](/blog/make-com-automation-sign-ups-one-list).

## A lead follow up system, so nobody gets forgotten

A plan only works if you know who's due today. That's where the list comes in.

Keep one table, with one row per person. [Airtable](https://www.airtable.com) is what we use, and we went through the columns in [a simple CRM for small business](/blog/simple-crm-for-small-business). Ours has a stage for each person and a column that shows how many days they've been waiting.

Then look at it each morning.

![The morning follow-up: look at your list, make a short list of who to reply to with a reply to start from, reply in your own words, then move them to the next stage](/images/blog/how-to-follow-up-with-leads/morning-follow-up.png)

We ask [Claude](https://www.anthropic.com/claude) to read the table for us. It tells us who's new and who's been waiting, flags anyone waiting 3 days or more, and writes a reply to start from. It never sends anything. We read it, change it and send it ourselves.

You don't need Claude for this. Sort your table by who's waited longest, and start from the top.

> **Try this:** tonight, write down everyone who has got in touch in the last 30 days. Next to each name, write the last time you replied. Send one short message to whoever has waited longest.

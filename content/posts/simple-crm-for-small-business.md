Someone comments on your post. Someone else fills in your website form. A third person sends a DM on a Sunday night. A week later you can't remember who asked for what, and two of them have gone quiet.

They were interested. They just got lost between three different places.

A simple CRM for small business fixes that. Not a big tool with fifty buttons. One list, in one place, that you own.

> **In plain words:** CRM just means a list of the people who find you, and where each one is up to. If you keep it in one table, you have a CRM.

## Why leads get lost when they live in five places

Most small businesses already have the names. They're just spread out. Some are in Instagram messages, some in an email inbox, some in a notes app, some in your head.

Each place works on its own. The trouble starts when you need to answer one simple question: who should I reply to today? Nobody can answer that across five apps. So the quiet ones get missed, and they're often the ones who were ready to buy.

What helps is one table that every door leads into.

![The steps to set up one list: make one table called Leads, add the columns, add a stage, add yourself as the first row, then the rest of the system can fill it](/images/blog/simple-crm-for-small-business/one-list-flow.png)

## What a simple CRM for small business needs

Ours is an Airtable table called Leads. It started small and it's still small. These are the columns that do the work:

| Column | What it's for |
| :--- | :--- |
| First name | So every reply starts with their name |
| Email | The one thing you need to reach them, on any app or none |
| Platform | Where they came from: Instagram, TikTok, your website |
| Date in | The day they found you |
| Consent to email | Did they say yes to hearing from you? |
| Stage | Where they're up to |
| What they said | Their words, so you know what they need |

That's it to start. We added a few more later, like a "Days waiting" count, once we knew we needed them. Start with fewer columns than you think. You can always add one.

![A sample Leads table: three people with first name, email, the door they came through, the date in, yes to emails, and their stage](/images/blog/simple-crm-for-small-business/one-list-table.png)

## A stage for every person

The stage is what turns a list of names into something you can act on. Ours has these:

![The stages a person moves through: New, Free thing sent, Replied, Conversation, Call booked, then Client](/images/blog/simple-crm-for-small-business/stages.png)

Every morning you can filter by stage and see who's waiting. Someone sitting on "Replied" for three days needs an answer today. Someone on "New" may just need the thing they asked for.

We also keep a "Not now" stage. Some people aren't ready, and that's fine. They stay on the list instead of disappearing.

## How to set it up in Airtable

You can do this tonight. No code.

1. **Make a free Airtable account** and a new base. Call it something plain, like "My business".
2. **Add one table called Leads.** If you'd rather start from a template, Airtable has a [lightweight CRM template](https://www.airtable.com/templates/lightweight-crm/expGObpa2AGjv2Z8t) you can trim down.
3. **Add the columns** from the table above. Make Email an email field and Stage a single select, with your stages as the options.
4. **Add yourself as the first row.** You're the test person. If your own row looks right, the setup works.
5. **Add the people you already have.** Go through your DMs and inbox once and add each person, with the stage they're really at.

> **Good to know:** Airtable has a free plan, and a small list fits on it. Check [Airtable's plans page](https://support.airtable.com/docs/airtable-plans) for what's included today, because it changes.

## Let the table fill itself

Typing names in manually is fine for the first week. After that it becomes another task you skip.

So the next step is joining the doors to the table. Our website forms already land in Leads on their own, through a tool called Make. We showed exactly how in [website forms to Airtable with Make](/blog/website-forms-to-airtable-with-make). If you don't have a website, an [Airtable form](https://support.airtable.com/articles/8588280258-getting-started-with-airtable-form-views) writes straight into the table, and we walked through that in [a free way to catch every enquiry from your posts](/blog/the-zero-dollar-owned-pipeline-for-creators).

Once the rows come in by themselves, the table stops being a chore. It's just where you look each morning.

> **Try this:** before you build anything else, add the last ten people who messaged you. Give each one a stage. You'll see straight away who you forgot to answer.

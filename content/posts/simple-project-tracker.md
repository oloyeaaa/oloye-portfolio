You're halfway through a job. Then a good idea turns up, and you start on it. A week later you've got two half-finished things and a third idea on the way.

A simple project tracker stops that. Every new idea goes into one list with a status, and you get back to the job in front of you.

> **In plain words:** a project tracker is a list of the things you want to do, with a word next to each one that says where it is. That's all it has to be.

## Why new ideas stop the work you've already started

New ideas feel better than finishing. The new one is clean and exciting. The old one has the boring last bits left.

So the old one waits. Then a customer asks when it'll be done, and you've moved on to something else.

The fix isn't to stop having ideas. It's to give them somewhere to wait.

## How our idea table works

We keep a table called the Roadmap. Every new idea for something to build goes in it as **Parked**, and nothing on it gets built until Oloye says so. That rule came in on 5 November, when we decided to finish what we had and stop starting new things.

It has more than 30 ideas in it now. Most are parked, a few have been dropped, and only a few are being built.

![A simple project tracker: a new idea goes in as Parked, then Next up, Building and Built, and anything you decide against goes to Dropped](/images/blog/simple-project-tracker/idea-statuses.png)

Each idea moves through five statuses:

| Status | What it means |
| :--- | :--- |
| Parked | Written down, not now |
| Next up | Chosen to start when the current job is done |
| Building | Being worked on now |
| Built | Done and in use |
| Dropped | Decided against, kept so you don't think of it again from scratch |

The **Dropped** one matters more than it looks. When the same idea comes back a month later, you can see why you said no last time.

## Set up a simple project tracker in one evening

You can use a spreadsheet, a notes app or [Airtable](https://www.airtable.com/templates). We use Airtable because our [simple CRM](/blog/simple-crm-for-small-business) and other lists live there too.

**Step 1: Make one table.** One row per idea.

**Step 2: Add a status column.** In Airtable, use a [single select field](https://support.airtable.com/docs/single-select-field) with the five statuses above. In a spreadsheet, a drop-down does the same job.

**Step 3: Add three more columns.**

- **What it is:** one or two lines.
- **Why it matters:** who it helps, or what it would save you.
- **Date added:** so you can see how long it's waited.

**Step 4: Put every idea in as Parked.** The ones in your head, the ones on scraps of paper, the ones in your phone notes. Get them all in one place.

**Step 5: Pick one thing to be Building.** Just one, if you can. Everything else waits.

> **Try this:** if you like seeing it as cards, Airtable has a [kanban view](https://support.airtable.com/docs/kanban-view) that puts each status in its own column. You drag a card from Parked to Next up when its turn comes.

## A weekly check so nothing gets lost

Parked doesn't mean forgotten. Once a week, look down the list and ask two questions:

1. Is the thing marked Building nearly done?
2. When it is, which one parked idea moves to Next up?

That's the whole check. It takes a few minutes, and it keeps your best ideas from going stale in a notebook.

> **Good to know:** a new idea that feels urgent can still wait a week. If it's still important at the next check, it'll still be there. Most of the time, the urge fades and the job you were on gets finished.

You open TikTok to post something. The camera's ready. Your mind goes blank.

So you scroll for ideas. An hour later, you've watched fifty videos and posted nothing.

If you don't know what to post on TikTok, you're not short of ideas. You're looking for them in the wrong place.

> **In plain words:** the best ideas for your posts don't come from other people's feeds. They come from what you already know.

## Why scrolling for ideas doesn't work

Scrolling feels like research. It isn't. It's the watching trap: you keep watching other people post, waiting for an idea to land, and it never feels like yours.

Even when you find one you like, it belongs to someone else's life. You try to copy it, it feels fake, and you don't post it.

The ideas that work come from your own week. The problem is that you're too close to them to see them.

## What to post on TikTok: start with four questions

Get a pen, or open your notes app. Answer these four questions in a few words each. Don't think too hard.

1. **What do you spend most of your day doing?** Your work, looking after people, a hobby, whatever fills your hours.
2. **What do people come to you for?** Advice, a fix, a recommendation, someone to talk things through with.
3. **What did you have to work out the hard way?** Money, a move, your health, an app that nobody explained.
4. **Who were you five years ago?** Someone a few steps behind you right now is that person.

Every answer is a post. Some are a whole series.

> **Good to know:** these are four of the eight questions on our free What to post page. We built it for exactly this problem.

A friend of mine wanted to start making videos. He was stressed and didn't know where to begin. So I asked him a few questions like these. What do you actually want to make? Who for? A few answers later, he had his topic: kids' stories and nursery rhymes. He didn't need to learn coding. He needed someone to ask the right questions.

## Five TikTok content ideas you can film today

Take your answers and try one of these. Each one works for almost any topic.

| Idea | What you say | Comes from |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| The question you always get | "People always ask me..." then answer it | Question 2 |
| The mistake you made | "I got this wrong for years..." then what you do now | Question 3 |
| What I do instead | "Most people do X. What I do instead is Y." | Questions 1 and 3 |
| Five years ago | "If you're where I was five years ago, start here." | Question 4 |
| A normal day | Show one real part of your day, and say one thing you learned | Question 1 |

None of these need a trend, a ring light or a script. They need one honest sentence from your own life.

> **Try this:** pick the idea that made you think "oh, I could say that". Film it on your phone today, in one take. Rough is fine.

## How to pick one idea and test it

You don't need a perfect plan. You need one idea and a week.

1. **Pick the idea that feels most like you.** Not the cleverest one. The one you could talk about without notes.
2. **Post about it three times this week.** The same idea from three angles: a question, a mistake, a tip.
3. **Watch what people ask.** The comments tell you what they want next. That's your next post.

TikTok's own [Creator Academy](https://www.tiktok.com/creator-academy/en/) has the basics on filming and posting, and the [help page on making videos](https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/creating-videos) walks you through the buttons. Use them for the how. Keep the what for your own life.

The app is open. The camera is ready. Then nothing. You scroll for a bit to get an idea, and an hour later you've watched forty videos and posted none.

If you don't know what to post on TikTok, you're not short of ideas. You're looking for them in the wrong place.

> **In plain words:** the best TikTok ideas for you come from your own life: your work, what people ask you, and what you learned the hard way. Not from other people's feeds.

## Why scrolling for TikTok ideas keeps you stuck

Scrolling feels like research. It isn't. We call it the watching trap: you watch other people post, and it feels like progress, but your own camera roll stays empty.

Their ideas came from their lives. When you copy them, the post feels borrowed, and it's hard to talk about something that isn't yours. So you stop, and scroll again.

The way out is to turn the camera around. Your ideas are sitting in your normal day.

## What to post on TikTok: start from what you already know

Grab a pen and answer these four questions. Short answers are fine.

1. **What do you spend most of your day doing?** A ward nurse, a school cook, a van driver and a mum of three each know things most people don't.
2. **What do people come to you for?** Friends who ask you to fix their CV, plan a trip or sort out a cake for a party are telling you what you're good at.
3. **What did you have to work out the hard way?** The mistake you made so someone else doesn't have to is a post.
4. **Who were you five years ago?** That person is who you're talking to. They're looking for what you know now.

Look at what you wrote. Each answer is a topic, and each topic has more posts in it than you'd think.

> **Try this:** take your answer to question 2. Write down the last three things someone asked you about it. That's three posts.

## Turn one answer into your first three posts

Pick one topic. Just one. Then make three short posts from it:

| Post | What it is | An example for someone who's great at birthday cakes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | The question people ask you most, answered | "How do you get the icing that smooth?" |
| 2 | The mistake you made, and what you do now | The cake that slid off the board, and the trick that stops it |
| 3 | One small thing they can try tonight | A two-minute fix for a lumpy buttercream |

You don't need a perfect plan. Post the three, then read the comments. The questions people ask under your posts are your next ideas. That's how a topic grows into a channel, one question at a time.

> **Good to know:** you don't have to pick a niche before you start. Your first few posts help you find it. TikTok's own [getting started guide](https://support.tiktok.com/en/getting-started) covers the app itself, so you can keep your energy for what you say.

## The page that asks the questions for you

Writing your own answers works. But a lot of people freeze at a blank page too, so we built one that asks the questions for you.

It's called What to post. It asks 8 quick questions, most of them taps, about your day, what people come to you for and what you learned the hard way. Then [Claude](https://www.anthropic.com/claude) uses your answers, and only your answers, to suggest ideas in four parts:

You pick the ones you like, and your plan comes to your email as a PDF. Because it starts from your own answers, the ideas sound like you, not like everyone else's feed.
